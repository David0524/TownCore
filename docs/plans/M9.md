## Where we start

  - `images` already records kind, license, attribution, `ai_generated` and `approved_by`. A database check refuses an AI image without a person's approval.
  - The beat defaults already name `reader-photo`, `business-photo` and `ai-illustration`. The router skips them until they're built.
- **Storage:** the mail platform keeps files in S3 (`MAIL_S3_BUCKET`), but AWS isn't deployed yet. `sharp` is installed (through Next).

## Design

### 1. Photo storage
- **Behind a small interface:**
  - S3 (the mail bucket, under `photos/`) when AWS is configured;
  - otherwise a local folder (development and CI).
- **Every upload is re-encoded with `sharp`:**
  - to JPEG, at most 2000 px on the long side;
  - **all metadata stripped (EXIF, GPS)**, so a reader's location never leaks;
  - files that aren't a real JPEG, PNG or WebP image are refused;
  - 12 MB at most.
- **Readers see an approved photo** through `/photos/<id>.jpg` on the town's own domain, served from storage. Pending and rejected photos are never served.

### 2. Reader and contributor photos (§11c)
- **Upload form:** `/photos` on each town site (posting to `/photos/send`), a plain HTML form with a honeypot and rate limits, like newsletter signups. It asks for:
  - photo;
  - caption;
  - where and when it was taken;
  - the credit line ("Photo: Pat Reader");
  - name and email (never shown);
  - the **license grant**, which must be checked.
- **The license grant:** a non-exclusive, royalty-free license for the site to publish the photo, with the credit, on the site, in its newsletter and on its social pages. The photographer keeps the copyright. They confirm they took it, and that it shows no one under 18 who could be identified and no private home's address. The grant's text and version are stored with the submission, with the IP and time.
- **Review:** staff approve or reject in **Admin → Photos** (audited). Approving needs alt text written by the reviewer from what the photo shows.
- **Use:** approved photos fill `reader-photo` for community and weather stories in that town, newest first, never twice in a week. The credit shows under the photo.
- **Contributors:** the same form and grant. A "pay per photo" program comes later (§11c).

### 3. Business photos in the claim flow
- The owner portal gets **Photos**: up to 5 per listing, with the same re-encoding and a business license grant (the business owns or may license the photo).
- Staff approve each one in Admin → Photos.
- Approved photos show on the business page and fill `business-photo` for business-opening stories about that listing.

### 4. AI illustration for guides
- **The code path:**
  - `ai-illustration` for guides behind an `IllustrationProvider` interface;
  - a prompt that asks for a clearly illustrative, non-photographic image with no people and no real place shown as a photo;
  - the label "Illustration (AI-generated)" on the page and in the alt text;
  - `ai_generated` set;
  - **staff approval before it shows** (the database already enforces it).
- **No provider is configured:** an image model is a new paid service the owner must choose (D-M9-4). Until then guides fall back to stock, then the typographic card.

## Decisions (taken overnight; the owner may change any)

| # | Question | Taken |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D-M9-1 | Storage | S3 under the mail bucket when AWS is configured; a local folder otherwise. |
| D-M9-2 | Reader license | Non-exclusive, royalty-free, site plus newsletter plus social pages, credit always shown; the photographer keeps the copyright. Grant text versioned and stored. |
| D-M9-3 | Moderation | Every photo needs staff approval and reviewer-written alt text. No automatic publishing. |
| D-M9-4 | AI illustration provider | **None until the owner picks one** (new paid service, §14). The path is built and labeled; guides use stock or cards meanwhile. |
| D-M9-5 | Privacy | Metadata (including GPS) stripped on upload; submitter name and email never shown; photos of identifiable minors refused by the grant and by the reviewer. |
